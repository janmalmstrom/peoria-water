Water Extraction & Structural Drying

What equipment is used to dry out a house after water damage?

By Marcus Webb

Quick Answer:

Restoration contractors use industrial dehumidifiers, air movers, moisture meters, and thermal imaging cameras to dry a water-damaged house to IICRC S500 standards.

Key Takeaways

  • Industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers remove far more moisture per day than any consumer-grade unit from a hardware store.
  • Air movers create turbulent airflow that accelerates evaporation from wet structural materials — without them, dehumidifiers alone cannot dry a structure efficiently.
  • Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras are used to find hidden pockets of water inside walls, subfloors, and ceiling cavities that look dry on the surface.
  • Daily moisture readings logged by the contractor create the documentation trail that insurance adjusters — including those at Pekin Insurance and Country Financial — need to approve claims.

Drying Equipment at a Glance

  • Industrial dehumidifiers — extract moisture from the air after evaporation
  • Air movers (axial & centrifugal fans) — push evaporation from wet surfaces and structural materials
  • Moisture meters (pin & pinless) — measure moisture content inside walls, floors, and framing
  • Thermal imaging cameras — detect temperature differentials that reveal hidden wet areas
  • Truck-mounted and portable extractors — remove standing water before drying begins
  • Negative air machines / air scrubbers — filter airborne particulates, required for Category 2 and 3 losses
  • Desiccant dehumidifiers — used in cold Peoria winters when refrigerant units lose efficiency

Why Consumer Equipment Can't Do This Job

Every big-box hardware store in Peoria rents dehumidifiers. A homeowner dealing with a burst pipe in January might reasonably wonder whether renting a few units and running box fans is good enough. The short answer: it isn't, and the gap between consumer and commercial equipment is larger than most people expect.

A standard residential dehumidifier typically removes roughly 30–70 pints of moisture per day under ideal conditions. A commercial low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifier — the type used on professional restoration jobs — typically removes 150 to over 200 pints per day and operates effectively at lower grain levels, meaning it keeps pulling moisture out of the air even as the relative humidity drops. Multiple units are staged strategically throughout the affected area based on square footage and material type, following the psychrometric calculations required by IICRC S500, the industry standard for water damage restoration.

The fan situation is similarly mismatched. Household fans push air around a room. Commercial air movers — both axial and centrifugal types — direct high-velocity laminar or turbulent airflow directly across wet surfaces and into wall cavities. That surface airflow is what converts liquid moisture into vapor so the dehumidifier has something to capture. Run dehumidifiers without air movers and you're fighting the problem at half speed. Run air movers without dehumidifiers and you're moving moisture from one surface to another.

How Moisture Detection Equipment Finds the Water You Can't See

Water follows the path of least resistance. In a Peoria home with a burst pipe — the kind of freeze-thaw failure common in January and February when temperatures swing from single digits to the mid-40s within 24 hours — water from a wall cavity can travel inside the framing, wick into subfloor sheathing, migrate along a top plate, and show up in a room 20 feet from the original break. The surface you can see and touch tells you almost nothing about the actual scope of the loss.

Pin and Pinless Moisture Meters

Restoration contractors use two types of moisture meters. Pin meters drive small probes into drywall, wood framing, or subfloor to measure electrical resistance — wet materials conduct electricity differently than dry ones. Pinless meters use electromagnetic sensors to scan deeper into materials without penetration, useful for hardwood floors and areas where surface damage needs to be minimized. Structural drying is complete only when readings return to normal dry standards for each material type, as defined in IICRC S500 — typically 12–17% moisture content for dimensional lumber in Central Illinois conditions.

Thermal Imaging Cameras

Infrared cameras detect temperature differentials, not moisture directly. Wet materials evaporate and cool; they show up as cooler areas on a thermal scan compared to dry surrounding materials. A trained contractor can sweep a basement ceiling in East Peoria or a Peoria Heights bungalow with cast iron drain lines and identify exactly where water has migrated behind drywall or above tile without opening up every wall. This matters enormously for scoping a claim accurately — thermal imaging provides visual documentation that Xactimate estimates can reference and that adjusters at carriers like State Farm or Pekin Insurance can review.

💡 Tip: Ask the restoration contractor to photograph the moisture meter readings at initial assessment and at each daily check-in. Those dated readings — not a contractor's verbal assurance — are what your insurance adjuster will want to see to confirm the drying scope was necessary and complete.

Extraction Equipment: What Happens Before the Drying Begins

Dehumidifiers and air movers cannot do their jobs efficiently if standing water is still present. The first step on any water damage call is extraction, and the difference between professional and consumer extraction is substantial.

Truck-mounted extractors carry water removal equipment powered by the vehicle engine, generating suction that portable units typically cannot match. They're used for heavy standing water events — a finished basement in Dunlap with several inches of water from a sump pump failure, for example, or a Category 3 sewage backup in an older Peoria Heights home. Portable extractors are used in areas the truck unit can't reach: upper floors, tight closets, or commercial spaces.

For hardwood floors specifically, contractors often use specialty floor mat drying systems — mats that lie flat on the flooring surface and use suction to pull moisture out through the finish rather than allowing it to dry from the top down. This can be the difference between saving a hardwood floor and replacing it, which matters considerably in the older Craftsman and bungalow homes common north of War Memorial Drive in Peoria Heights.

Negative Air Machines and Air Scrubbers: When Contamination Is Involved

Not every water damage event involves clean water. IICRC S500 classifies water damage into three categories: Category 1 (clean water, such as a supply line break), Category 2 (gray water with some contamination, such as washing machine discharge or aquarium overflow), and Category 3 (black water — sewage, floodwater from outside sources, or standing water that has degraded). For Category 2 and Category 3 losses, the equipment list expands.

Negative air machines create containment pressure in the affected area, preventing contaminated air from migrating to unaffected parts of the structure. HEPA air scrubbers filter particulates — including mold spores — from the air inside the containment zone. The EPA's mold guidelines and the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard both specify containment protocols for situations where mold growth is confirmed or likely.

⚠️ Watch out: Sewage backup cleanup (Category 3) requires personal protective equipment, containment, and EPA-registered antimicrobial application — not just extraction and fans. Standard mop-and-shop-vac cleanup of a sewage backup leaves behind pathogens. Sewer backup coverage is typically a separate endorsement on a standard homeowners policy; confirm your coverage before assuming the claim is straightforward.

Cold-Weather Equipment: Desiccant Dehumidifiers in a Peoria Winter

Refrigerant dehumidifiers — the standard LGR units used in most residential restoration — typically lose significant efficiency when air temperature drops below 45–50°F. In a Peoria winter, this matters. A pipe that bursts at 2 a.m. in January may have flooded a basement that's sitting at 38°F by the time the contractor arrives. Running LGR units in those conditions produces substandard results.

Professional contractors managing cold-weather losses in Central Illinois often switch to desiccant dehumidifiers, which use silica gel or other desiccant materials to absorb moisture through a chemical process rather than refrigerant condensation. Desiccant units remain effective down to freezing and below, making them a suitable equipment choice for unheated spaces — detached garages, crawl spaces, or basement areas where heating the space isn't immediately feasible. Some contractors use temporary heating equipment in parallel to bring the space into an acceptable temperature range for LGR units; the right approach depends on the specific structure, loss size, and timeline.

What the Equipment Log Means for Your Insurance Claim

Every piece of drying equipment placed on a job — dehumidifier model and capacity, number of air movers, moisture meter readings by location, thermal images with timestamps — should be logged daily. This documentation serves two purposes: it confirms that drying is progressing toward the IICRC S500 standard, and it provides the paper trail that justifies the equipment cost on your insurance claim.

Xactimate, the estimating software used by adjusters at most major carriers including State Farm, Country Financial, and USAA, prices equipment by the day based on documented quantity and type. An adjuster cannot approve what isn't documented. Contractors who skip the daily moisture log — or who remove equipment before readings confirm dryness — create claim disputes and leave homeowners exposed to secondary damage like mold growth, which the EPA notes can begin within 24 to 48 hours on wet organic materials.

Pekin Insurance, headquartered in Pekin, IL, is known among local contractors for having adjusters who are familiar with Central Illinois restoration pricing and documentation norms. A properly documented drying log tends to move through review more efficiently than a generic summary from an out-of-area contractor who flew in after a storm.

Bottom Line

Drying a water-damaged Peoria home to IICRC S500 standard requires industrial LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers, commercial air movers, thermal imaging cameras, and calibrated moisture meters — not hardware store rentals. In Central Illinois winters, desiccant dehumidifiers are often necessary in cold unheated spaces. Contractors use and document all of this equipment daily, producing the moisture logs that Peoria-area insurance adjusters need to process your claim. Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of dehumidifier is used for water damage restoration?

Professional restoration contractors use low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers, which remove 150–200+ pints of moisture per day — far more than residential units. In cold Peoria winters, when basement temperatures drop below 45–50°F, desiccant dehumidifiers are used instead because refrigerant units lose efficiency at low temperatures.

How many air movers are needed to dry a flooded room?

Equipment placement is calculated using psychrometric formulas defined in IICRC S500, accounting for square footage, material type, and moisture levels — not a simple per-room rule. A typical residential loss might involve one air mover per 50–70 square feet of affected floor area, with additional units directed into wall cavities and under cabinetry.

Can I use my own dehumidifier and fans instead of hiring a contractor?

Consumer equipment removes a fraction of the moisture that commercial units extract per day, and without moisture meters and thermal imaging, you won't know when materials are actually dry or where hidden water has migrated. Attempting DIY drying can leave concealed moisture that leads to mold growth within 24–48 hours on wet organic materials, according to IICRC S520.

What is a thermal imaging camera used for in water damage restoration?

Infrared cameras detect temperature differentials between wet and dry materials — wet areas evaporate and cool, showing up differently on thermal scans. Contractors use them to locate hidden water inside walls, subfloors, and ceiling cavities without opening up every surface, which keeps repair costs lower and provides visual documentation for insurance adjusters.

Does the drying equipment have to be on-site for the entire claim?

Yes — equipment stays on-site until moisture meter readings confirm that all affected structural materials have returned to normal dry-standard levels as defined by IICRC S500. Removing equipment before those targets are met to save on equipment costs is a shortcut that risks secondary mold damage and creates claim liability.

What equipment is used for sewage backup cleanup specifically?

Category 3 losses (sewage backup) require all standard drying equipment plus negative air machines for containment, HEPA air scrubbers to filter contaminated airborne particulates, and EPA-registered antimicrobial application. Contractors also use personal protective equipment; sewage backup is a biohazard situation, not just a water extraction job.

How does the equipment log help with my insurance claim in Peoria?

Insurance adjusters using Xactimate pricing price drying equipment by type, quantity, and number of days on-site — all of which must be documented with daily moisture logs to be approved. Pekin Insurance adjusters, who are familiar with Central Illinois restoration norms, process documented claims more efficiently than undocumented ones from out-of-area contractors.

How does cold weather in Peoria affect the drying equipment used?

Standard LGR refrigerant dehumidifiers lose significant efficiency when air temperature drops below 45–50°F — common in Peoria basements during January and February freeze-thaw events. Restoration contractors managing cold-weather losses switch to desiccant dehumidifiers, which remain effective at or below freezing, or use temporary heating to bring the space into the range where refrigerant units work properly.
