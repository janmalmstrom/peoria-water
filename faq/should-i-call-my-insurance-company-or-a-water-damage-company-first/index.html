Emergency Response & First Steps

Should I call my insurance company or a water damage company first?

By Marcus Webb

Quick Answer:

Call a water damage company first — stopping active damage takes priority over paperwork, and most insurers expect mitigation to begin immediately.

Key Takeaways

  • Delaying water extraction even by a few hours allows moisture to penetrate deeper into wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation — increasing both the damage scope and the final claim amount.
  • Mold can begin growing on wet organic materials within 24–48 hours per the IICRC S520 standard, which means every hour spent on hold with an insurance hotline is an hour closer to a secondary mold claim.
  • Most standard homeowners policies include a "duty to mitigate" clause — failure to stop active damage promptly can give your insurer grounds to reduce or deny a portion of your claim.
  • Peoria-area insurers like Pekin Insurance and Country Financial are familiar with local restoration contractors and the documentation formats they produce — starting mitigation first actually strengthens your claim file.

Why the sequence matters when water is actively damaging your home

When a pipe bursts at 1 a.m. in January — a scenario that plays out dozens of times every winter across Peoria Heights and Dunlap as Central Illinois temperatures swing from single digits to above freezing in under 24 hours — your first instinct may be to call State Farm or Pekin Insurance to start the claim. That instinct is understandable, but it's backwards. Water does not wait for claim numbers.

Structural drying under the IICRC S500 standard is a time-sensitive process. Clean water (Category 1) that sits against drywall, hardwood flooring, or dimensional lumber for even a few hours begins wicking upward and laterally into material assemblies. What starts as a localized pipe failure in a basement utility room can become a full subfloor replacement if extraction and drying equipment isn't on-site quickly. The contractors we connect Peoria homeowners with are available 24/7 specifically because the first hours of a water loss are the most consequential.

The right call sequence for Peoria water damage

  • Step 1: Shut off the water source if safe to do so (main shutoff, appliance valve, or notify building maintenance)
  • Step 2: Call a local water damage contractor immediately — (309) 555-0100

  • Step 4: Document visible damage with your phone before any work begins

What your insurance policy's "duty to mitigate" actually means

Most Illinois homeowners policies — including those issued by State Farm (headquartered in Bloomington, less than 90 minutes from Peoria), Country Financial, Pekin Insurance, and national carriers like Allstate and USAA — include language that requires the policyholder to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a covered loss occurs. This is called the duty to mitigate.

In practical terms: if an adjuster later determines that you waited six hours to call anyone while water continued spreading across your finished basement, the carrier may argue that a portion of the resulting damage was preventable. This doesn't mean they'll deny your entire claim — but it does give them a basis to reduce the payout for damage that occurred after the initial event.

⚠️ Watch out: Do not let a contractor begin demolition, disposal, or any work beyond emergency stabilization before your insurer has been notified and, if required, has had an opportunity to inspect. Premature tear-out can complicate the claim. Emergency extraction and drying equipment can — and should — be deployed before the adjuster arrives; cosmetic repairs and structural replacements should wait.

Calling a restoration contractor first does not mean cutting your insurer out of the process. It means fulfilling your policy obligations by stopping the bleeding, then immediately looping in your carrier. The contractors we work with produce detailed moisture logs, thermal imaging reports, and equipment placement records — the kind of documentation Pekin Insurance adjusters and other regional carriers expect when reviewing a water claim.

When to call your insurer first — the limited exceptions

There are a small number of situations where contacting your insurance company before a restoration crew makes sense or is actually required:

  • You're not sure if the damage is covered: If your basement has been seeping groundwater for months and you're uncertain whether this is a maintenance issue or a sudden loss, a quick call to your carrier before spending money on extraction is reasonable. Note that chronic seepage and Illinois River overbank flooding are generally not covered under standard homeowners policies — the former is a maintenance issue, the latter requires an NFIP flood insurance policy.
  • Your policy requires prior authorization for certain work: Some policies — particularly older commercial property policies — may require adjuster approval before restoration begins. Review your declarations page or call your agent if unsure.
  • The damage is minor and contained: A single appliance leak caught within minutes that hasn't reached structural materials may not warrant a claim at all. Minor losses below your deductible are often better handled out of pocket to protect your claims history.

The mold clock: why the 24–48 hour window changes everything

Per the IICRC S520 standard, mold can begin growing on wet organic materials — drywall paper, wood framing, carpet backing, insulation — within 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature and humidity conditions. Peoria homes in the $200,000–$400,000 range commonly have finished basements with drywall, engineered wood subfloor, and carpet — exactly the materials most susceptible to rapid mold colonization.

A water damage claim is manageable. A water damage claim that also requires mold remediation under IICRC S520 protocols is substantially more expensive — small-area mold remediation in the Peoria market typically ranges from $1,200–$3,500; large structural mold jobs typically range from $6,000–$15,000 or more. And mold remediation coverage varies by policy; some carriers treat it as a separate coverage limit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my homeowners insurance cover water damage if I call the contractor before filing a claim?

Yes — calling a restoration contractor first does not jeopardize your claim. In fact, most standard homeowners policies require you to take reasonable steps to stop further damage (the "duty to mitigate"), so prompt mitigation supports your claim rather than undermining it. Notify your insurer as soon as the contractor is on-site or even while you're waiting for them to arrive.

How long do I have to file a water damage claim in Illinois?

Illinois law requires insurers to acknowledge a claim within 10 business days of notification, but your policy likely specifies that you must report losses "promptly" or within a defined window. Check your declarations page — but as a practical matter, same-day or next-business-day notification after emergency mitigation begins is the standard expectation. Waiting days or weeks to report a loss creates complications.

What if my adjuster hasn't approved the work yet — can the contractor still start extraction?

Emergency extraction and drying equipment can and should be deployed before adjuster arrival; this is standard practice under IICRC S500 and expected by carriers. What should wait for adjuster review is permanent repair work — replacing drywall, flooring, or structural components. The extraction and drying phase is emergency mitigation, not repair, and insurers distinguish between the two.

Does Pekin Insurance require a preferred vendor or can I choose my own contractor?

Most Illinois homeowners policies, including Pekin Insurance policies, allow you to choose your own licensed contractor. Your insurer may suggest a preferred vendor, but you are generally not required to use them. Under the Illinois Home Repair and Remodeling Act (HRRA), any contractor doing residential work over $1,000 must provide a written contract — insist on this regardless of who you hire.

Is basement flooding from the Illinois River covered by my homeowners policy?

No. Illinois River overbank flooding — water entering your home from the ground or from rising exterior floodwater — is not covered by standard homeowners insurance. That requires a separate National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policy. However, if your basement flooded because a pipe burst, a sump pump failed, or an appliance discharged, that is typically a different category of loss that standard policies do cover. Sewer backup coverage often requires a separate endorsement.

Can I document the damage myself before the contractor arrives?

Absolutely — and you should. Walk through the affected areas and take photos and video of all visible water, damaged materials, and the source of the loss before any cleanup begins. This record supplements the contractor's professional moisture mapping and thermal imaging report. Do not move or dispose of damaged materials before the contractor documents them, and do not attempt to use a household wet-vac as a substitute for professional extraction on a significant loss.

What does the IICRC S500 standard have to do with my insurance claim?

The IICRC S500 is the industry standard for professional water damage restoration — it defines drying goals, equipment requirements, and documentation protocols. Most adjusters at carriers like State Farm, Allstate, USAA, and Pekin Insurance use Xactimate software to price claims, and that pricing is built around S500-compliant work. Contractors who follow S500 protocols produce documentation (moisture logs, drying records, equipment placement diagrams) that aligns directly with what adjusters need to process a claim efficiently.
