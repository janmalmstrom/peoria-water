## 13. Local Intelligence

### Neighborhoods & Areas
- **Peoria Heights** (north, 1920s-1940s bungalows): galvanized/copper pipes at end of life, basement flooding common
- **Dunlap** (northwest suburbs, newer): sump pump failures in spring, finished basements = high damage cost
- **Chillicothe** (north, Illinois River adjacent): groundwater table issues, basement seepage common spring/fall
- **East Peoria** (Tazewell County): Caterpillar supplier plants + residential mix, commercial water damage calls
- **Pekin** (south, Tazewell County): Pekin Insurance HQ — local adjusters know local restoration rates
- **Morton** (southeast, "Pumpkin Capital"): newer subdivisions, finished basements, high appliance failure frequency

### Local Price Benchmarks (Peoria market, 2024–2025)
- Water extraction only (<500 sq ft): $400–$900
- Full water damage restoration (average residential): $2,800–$6,500
- Category 3 / sewage backup cleanup: $5,000–$12,000
- Mold remediation (small area): $1,200–$3,500

### Regional Specifics
- **Illinois River flooding:** Spring flooding from snowmelt is recurring. Interior home damage from burst pipes ≠ river flooding — covered by standard homeowners insurance. River overbank flooding requires NFIP.
- **Freeze-thaw cycle:** Peoria averages 20+ days below freezing per winter. Below zero to 40°F in 24 hours = primary cause of pipe bursts. January–February = peak season.
- **Caterpillar Inc. HQ:** Engineers and middle-management homeowners with good insurance and high expectations for professional documentation.
- **OSF HealthCare & Methodist Medical Center:** Two major hospital systems. Medical office buildings = commercial water damage clients.
- **Older housing stock:** 45%+ of Peoria homes built before 1980. Cast iron drain lines, galvanized supply pipes, older water heaters.
- **Illinois HRRA:** Contractors doing residential work >$1,000 must provide written contracts. Mention as trust signal.
